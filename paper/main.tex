\begin{abstract}
Industrial detection studies often conflate informative routing, arithmetic sparsity, accuracy, and deployed efficiency. We audit them separately using WSR, which ranks P3 features with fixed Haar responses and refines an exact top-$k$ budget. On DsPCBSD+, seven paired runs give 46.69$\pm$0.72 AP$_{50:95}$ versus 46.54$\pm$0.36 for YOLO11s; the 0.15-point difference is nonsignificant ($p=0.8125$). Three descriptive DeepPCB pairs gain 4.20 points but increase alarms on defect-free templates. At a 25\% budget, routes are enriched inside boxes by 3.16$\times$/3.55$\times$ on the two datasets, although spatial priors explain much of the primary enrichment and matched convolution reproduces the validation gain. WSR adds 13.3K parameters and 0.038 GFLOPs but is 1.18--1.21$\times$ slower. Post-hoc negative-aware training lowers DeepPCB board-FPR from 53.5\% to 27.5\%; an exploratory paired-reference detector reaches 1.4\% and 0.959 F1 under a changed input contract. Thus WSR verifies image-dependent allocation, not a reliable accuracy or systems advantage, and exposes why route overlap, FLOPs, positive-only AP, and component attribution need separate controls.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Automated optical inspection must find small, low-contrast defects among repeated traces and pads without alarming on normal boards. The challenge is not only representation: an industrial detector must retain thin discontinuities, distinguish them from legitimate high-frequency copper patterns, and sustain predictable behavior at a fixed operating point. Conventional positive-only benchmarks emphasize localization on defective images, whereas a deployed line also sees many normal boards for which one false detection may trigger manual review. Sparse frequency-aware modules appear attractive, yet route overlap need not improve detection, nominal sparsity need not accelerate hardware, positive-only AP can hide false alarms, and generic capacity can mimic a specialized cue.

We study these distinctions through wavelet-conditioned sparse routing (WSR). Fixed Haar responses rank P3 locations, and gather--refine--scatter applies a channel transform to $k=\operatorname{round}(\rho HW)$ tokens rather than masking a dense transform. The architecture therefore poses four separable hypotheses: selected cells should be defect-enriched; that allocation should improve held-out accuracy; its sparse arithmetic should reduce measured latency; and any gain should depend on the proposed cues rather than added capacity. We treat WSR as an auditable probe, testing same-budget route controls, paired-seed accuracy, order-balanced latency, negative inputs, and matched components. This claim-by-claim design is important because a plausible heat map, a favorable seed, or a lower FLOP count cannot establish the other hypotheses.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) An exact-budget P3/8 probe separates arithmetic sparsity from realized speed. (2) Random, spatial, activation, fixed-Haar, cross-image, matched-convolution, and fusion controls test routing and attribution. (3) Defect-free templates expose a blind spot of positive-only PCB evaluation and separate target-only mitigation from changed-contract reference assistance. (4) A leakage-audited protocol reports null and adverse findings: the primary gain is nonsignificant and no variant passes the joint accuracy--latency gate.

\section{Related Work}
Detection spans Faster R-CNN/FPN~\cite{fasterrcnn,fpn}, set prediction~\cite{detr,deformabledetr}, and learned proposals~\cite{sparsercnn}. Their sparsity targets samples, queries, or multiscale proposals; WSR instead sparsifies one channel-refinement operator while preserving the detector's high-resolution feature lattice. Contemporary context uses pinned YOLOv10/11/12/26~\cite{yolov10,yolo11,yolov12,yolo26}, RT-DETRv2~\cite{rtdetrv2}, D-FINE~\cite{dfine}, DEIM~\cite{deim}, and RF-DETR~\cite{rfdetr} implementations. We do not interpret this cross-family comparison as an ablation: each official model retains its architecture-appropriate training recipe, so it locates WSR on the accuracy--size landscape rather than identifying a causal advantage.

PCB protocols differ materially. DeepPCB pairs aligned targets and clean templates~\cite{deeppcb}; TDD-Net uses an augmented six-class set~\cite{tddnet}; DsPCBSD+ supplies nine production-AOI classes~\cite{dspcbsd}; and PCB-IND adds real illumination and long-tail variation~\cite{pcbind} but postdates our frozen protocol. PCB-YOLO, YOLO-HMC, SRN-Net, and PCB-MMF use different splits, input contracts, or evaluators~\cite{pcbyolo,yolohmc,srnnet,pcbmmf}, so their headline results are not pooled with ours. This distinction matters because paired reference images can suppress board-specific texture but alter the information available at inference, while augmentation-derived test sets can inflate the apparent sample size. We therefore use only same-protocol runs for statistical comparisons and present recent methods as context.

Frequency transforms suppress aliasing, drive channel attention, or enhance detection~\cite{wavecnet,fcanet,hwanet,waveletyolo}. Conditional computation adapts depth, blocks, gates, or tokens~\cite{sact,sbnet,dynamicconv,tokenlearner,dynamicvit,evit}. These lines also warn against equating algorithmic sparsity with speed: scoring, sorting, memory movement, and irregular kernels may dominate a small skipped transform. WSR instead retains the dense P3 lattice and gathers exactly $k$ locations only for its channel MLP; our question is whether that frequency-conditioned budget is useful, unique, and fast under direct controls and device timing.

\section{Audited Routing Probe}
\subsection{Wavelet-Conditioned Routing}
Let $\mathbf X\in\mathbb R^{C\times H\times W}$ be the P3/8 feature. We split it into wave and refinement tensors $\mathbf X_w,\mathbf X_r$ with $C/2$ channels each, avoiding dense $C\times C$ projections. Fixed grouped stride-two Haar convolution gives
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{LL},\mathbf{LH},\mathbf{HL},\mathbf{HH})=\mathcal H(\mathbf X_w),
\end{equation}
using separable vectors $[1,1]/\sqrt2$ and $[-1,1]/\sqrt2$. We channel-average the absolute detail bands into $E_{hf}$, retain $E_{ll}=\operatorname{mean}_c|\mathbf{LL}|$, and form the local low-frequency residual $G_{ll}=|E_{ll}-\operatorname{AvgPool}_{3\times3}(E_{ll})|$. After upsampling and per-image normalization, a small convolutional router predicts
\begin{equation}
\mathbf S=R_\theta([\bar E_{hf};\bar E_{ll};\bar G_{ll}])
+\log(1+\bar E_{hf}+\bar G_{ll}).
\end{equation}
The additive fixed term supplies a meaningful initial ranking. The fixed filters are stored as nontrainable grouped-convolution buffers; odd feature sizes are reflect-padded at the lower or right boundary. $R_\theta$ is a $3\times3$ convolution from three cues to 16 channels, followed by normalization, SiLU, and a $1\times1$ projection. Flattening spatial locations, we select
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I=\operatorname{TopK}(\mathbf S,k),\quad
k=\min(HW,\max(4,\operatorname{round}(\rho HW))).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sparse Refinement and Fusion}
A learned softmax-normalized $3\times3$ kernel supplies dense depthwise context over $\mathbf X_r$; only values at $\mathcal I$ are gathered. For flattened tokens $\mathbf T$ and gathered context $\tilde{\mathbf T}_{\mathcal I}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf T'_{\mathcal I}=\mathbf T_{\mathcal I}+\sigma(\mathbf S_{\mathcal I})\odot
\operatorname{MLP}\!\left(\operatorname{LN}(\mathbf T_{\mathcal I}+\gamma(\tilde{\mathbf T}_{\mathcal I}-\mathbf T_{\mathcal I}))\right).
\end{equation}
The bias-free two-layer MLP has hidden width $\max(C/8,16)$; unrouted tokens remain unchanged before branch fusion. The context kernel is shared across channels, constrained by softmax to be nonnegative and sum to one, and applied with replicate padding. This avoids a dense unfold tensor while retaining local information at gathered positions. The MLP costs $O(\rho HWC^2)$ rather than $O(HWC^2)$, although the $O(9HWC)$ context and routing operations stay dense. Hard indices receive no gradient; the router learns only through selected confidence factors and detector losses, without a straight-through estimator or auxiliary routing loss. This gradient path motivates the fixed-router controls below.

Directional Haar magnitudes also produce a sigmoid wave gate $A_w$, giving $\mathbf X'_w=\mathbf X_w\odot(1+A_w)$. A two-way softmax gate uses global context, mean high-frequency energy, and selected-route confidence to fuse the branches:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf Y=\mathbf X+[\alpha_w\mathbf X'_w;\alpha_r\mathbf X'_r],
\qquad \alpha_w+\alpha_r=1.
\end{equation}
The formal model inserts one block at P3 with $\rho=0.25$. A later identity-preserving variant fuses near-zero-initialized branch deltas and replaces batch normalization in the router with group normalization; it is evaluated only in validation-locked engineering rounds and never replaces the formal test model.

\section{Audit Design and Experiments}
\subsection{Protocol}
DsPCBSD+~\cite{dspcbsd} has 10,259 images, 20,276 boxes, and nine classes. We split its 8,208 training images into 7,387/821 train/validation and lock the official 2,051-image validation set as test. DeepPCB~\cite{deeppcb} uses 850/150 official-training images and all 500 test targets. Formal AP uses only positive targets; clean templates are audited separately.

Repeated layouts may trigger it, but absent board/lot IDs prevent a categorical leakage-free claim.

Both models start from the same YOLO11s checkpoint and use 640-pixel input, at most 80 epochs, deterministic SGD with cosine decay, batch 8/nominal 64, patience 15, and matched augmentation. Pairing each WSR run with a baseline at the same seed removes part of the optimizer and data-order variation. We use seven paired DsPCBSD+ seeds; three DeepPCB pairs are descriptive. Shifted pretrained keys are remapped and runs below 99\% transfer are rejected. The prespecified P3/25\% model must pass a three-seed validation gate; test labels never select checkpoints or architectures, and post-hoc tracks cannot promote a candidate. We preserve this hierarchy even when a later diagnostic produces a larger number: registered test evidence, validation-only engineering, and exploratory mitigation answer different questions.

One pinned COCO evaluator~\cite{coco} retains AP, AR, size, and class metrics. We report mean$\pm$sample SD, paired 95\% $t$ intervals, exact two-sided Wilcoxon tests, and cross-dataset Holm correction. Three-seed component controls are validation-only and descriptive. Batch-one RTX 3090 latency uses one process, 12 alternating ABBA/BAAB cycles, and 600 synchronized observations per condition.

\subsection{Metrics and Evidential Roles}
For image $i$, let $M_i$ be the selected routing cells, $B_i$ the union of rasterized ground-truth box cells, and $N_i=H_iW_i$. We report route recall $r_i=|M_i\cap B_i|/|B_i|$ and enrichment
\begin{equation}
e_i=\frac{r_i}{|M_i|/N_i},
\end{equation}
then average by image; $e_i=1$ is uniform same-budget selection. Board-FPR is the fraction of clean templates with at least one retained detection, while FP/image preserves multiplicity; operating-point recall/F1 use class-matched IoU 0.5. Paired target-image AP$_{50:95}$ is primary. Route, latency, class, corruption, frequency, component, and negative-aware results are mechanism, diagnostic, validation-only, or post-hoc evidence and cannot replace it.

\subsection{Operational False-Alarm Audit}
DeepPCB's 500 clean templates expose a failure hidden by positive-only AP. At confidence 0.25, seed-13 WSR alarms on 56.6\% of boards (1.472 FP/image) versus 48.0\% (1.138); it is worse at all four thresholds (Fig.~\ref{fig:false_alarm}(a)). Three post-hoc negative-aware stages lower three-seed board-FPR from 53.5\% to 27.5\% at 0.946 recall.

At confidence 0.05/0.10/0.25/0.50, WSR/base board-FPR is 87.6/77.0/56.6/35.0\% versus 79.0/66.2/48.0/28.0\%; corrected board- and count-level tests remain significant. Aligned templates identify a failure mode, not production FPR.

Stages add a frozen 25\% template subset, all 850 training templates, then oversample the training-only hardest quarter. The seed-13 schedule is frozen for seeds 42/3407. AP rises 68.81$\rightarrow$72.44 and FPR falls 53.5\%$\rightarrow$27.5\%; a validation-calibrated 1\% cap transfers to 1.8--6.2\% FPR at only 0.790 mean recall.

\subsection{Auxiliary Robustness and Frequency Evidence}
Across eight corruptions and five severities on DeepPCB seed 13, WSR averages 46.51 AP versus 42.68 and wins 24/40 conditions. Gains concentrate in brightness, contrast, Gaussian noise, and JPEG; defocus, Poisson, and speckle regress. Clean-normalized retention is 65.6\%/64.1\%, indicating conditional, not general, robustness.

Input-Haar interventions give 67.43/66.22 AP for WSR/base under low-only reconstruction and 0.95/1.67 under high-only. Removing LH/HL/HH costs WSR 1.32/0.70/1.68 points versus 1.59/0.92/3.68. Low-frequency structure is essential, but this external single-checkpoint intervention cannot establish internal-branch causality (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}).

\subsection{Reproducibility and Decision Trace}
The audit separates immutable evidence from regenerated presentation. Dataset manifests record split counts and file hashes; standardized run records bind the model family, seed, checkpoint, evaluator output, and source revision; aggregate tables and figures are regenerated from those records rather than transcribed from console output. Acceptance checks reject incomplete evaluation files, inconsistent category mappings, and pretrained transfers below the stated threshold. Selection logs identify the metric and data role used at each stage: the pilot gate chooses the formal candidate from validation data, the locked test estimates its primary effect, and later component, robustness, and negative-aware studies retain diagnostic or exploratory labels. This trace prevents a stronger post-hoc variant from silently replacing the tested hypothesis.

Numerical reproducibility does not by itself imply inferential independence. Per-class, size, corruption, and threshold points reuse checkpoints and images, so they are reported as decompositions rather than additional samples. Conversely, paired seeds are the sampling unit for model comparison, and latency cycles are the unit for device timing. We retain exact seed-level values and confidence intervals even when they weaken the architectural narrative; this is necessary for distinguishing a stable mechanism from a stable detector improvement.

\section{Findings, Scope, and Limitations}
Four findings follow. Route enrichment is not an accuracy surrogate: spatial priors explain much of it, matched controls reproduce the validation trend, and the seven-seed interval crosses zero. Arithmetic sparsity is not systems efficiency because dense support and indexing make WSR slower. Positive-only AP hides normal-board alarms. Finally, full-versus-baseline ablation cannot attribute components when removing HF cues or adaptive fusion does not lower validation AP.

Consequently, route visualizations need same-budget spatial controls, FLOPs need order-balanced device timing, industrial benchmarks need negative operating points, and specialized modules need matched capacity and established plug-ins. A useful routing analysis should report both recall and enrichment because high recall is trivial at a large budget, and should compare against priors that use no test labels. Likewise, a latency claim should state batch size, precision, synchronization, order, and physical device instead of inferring runtime from parameters or FLOPs. The established-attention comparison remains incomplete: CBAM-, CoordAtt-, EMA-, and SimAM-style P3 controls were not run.

Reference assistance gives the best false-alarm trade-off but is post-hoc, single-seed, changed-contract, and tested on perturbations of known templates. Its 1.4\% estimate is therefore a feasibility result, not a production guarantee or a fair replacement for the target-only primary model. Further limits are three DeepPCB pairs, one GPU, incomplete comparator timing, one component-validation split, hard top-$k$, absent board/lot IDs, and unresolved similarity candidates. PCB-IND~\cite{pcbind} postdates the frozen protocol; future hypotheses and splits should precede tuning, and production claims require real negatives stratified by template, camera, illumination, and lot. Code and audit artifacts are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/master-abc/WSR-YOLO}; a complete archival release should additionally preserve frozen policies, per-image predictions, and obtainable checkpoints. Independent replication should preserve the paired-seed design and report failures as well as the selected configuration.

\section{Conclusion}
WSR shows that defect-enriched routes, low nominal FLOPs, accuracy, and deployment behavior do not move together. It concentrates P3 cells inside defects at little arithmetic cost, yet establishes neither reliable primary gain nor acceleration, and target-only false alarms remain high. The contribution is an auditable boundary result and evaluation template, not WSR superiority. Future work requires established attention controls, fused kernels, more seeds, group-safe splits, and preregistered reference-assisted tests on independent production negatives.
