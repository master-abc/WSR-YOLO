\begin{abstract}
Traditional image processing for industrial visual inspection relies on hand-crafted filtering, thresholding, and edge operators, whereas WSR-YOLO represents a modern deep-learning-based approach. We audit wavelet-conditioned sparse routing (WSR), which ranks P3 features with fixed Haar responses and refines an exact top-$k$ budget. On DsPCBSD+, seven paired runs yield 46.69$\pm$0.72 AP$_{50:95}$ versus 46.54$\pm$0.36 for YOLO11s; the 0.15-point difference is nonsignificant ($p=0.8125$). Three descriptive DeepPCB pairs gain 4.20 points but increase alarms on defect-free templates. At a 25\% budget, routes are enriched inside boxes by 3.16$\times$/3.55$\times$ on the two datasets, although spatial priors explain much of the primary enrichment and matched convolution reproduces the validation gain. WSR adds 13.3K parameters and 0.038 GFLOPs but is 1.18--1.21$\times$ slower. Negative-aware training lowers DeepPCB board-FPR from 53.5\% to 27.5\%; an exploratory paired-reference detector reaches 1.4\% and 0.959 F1 under a changed input contract. These results separate route overlap, arithmetic cost, accuracy, and deployment behavior instead of treating them as interchangeable evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Automated optical inspection must find small, low-contrast defects among repeated traces and pads without alarming on normal boards. Conventional positive-only benchmarks emphasize localization on defective images, while a production line also sees many normal boards for which one false detection may trigger review. Frequency-aware sparse modules are attractive, but a favorable route map or FLOP count does not establish accuracy, speed, or operational reliability.

We therefore use WSR as an auditable probe. Fixed Haar responses rank P3 locations, and gather--refine--scatter applies a channel transform only to $k=\operatorname{round}(\rho HW)$ tokens. We test four distinct questions: whether selected cells are defect-enriched, whether the allocation improves held-out accuracy, whether sparse arithmetic reduces measured latency, and whether any gain is unique to the proposed cues. The main contribution is a controlled evaluation that reports both positive and adverse results: same-budget routing controls, paired-seed accuracy, device timing, defect-free inputs, and matched-capacity components.

\section{Related Work}
Detection architectures span region proposals~\cite{fasterrcnn}, set prediction~\cite{detr}, sparse learned proposals~\cite{sparsercnn}, and compact YOLO/DETR families~\cite{yolo11,dfine}. PCB protocols differ materially: DeepPCB supplies aligned target/template pairs~\cite{deeppcb}, DsPCBSD+ has nine production-AOI classes~\cite{dspcbsd}, and PCB-IND adds illumination and long-tail variation~\cite{pcbind}. PCB-YOLO and YOLO-HMC use different evaluators~\cite{pcbyolo,yolohmc}, so headline scores are contextual.

Wavelets have been used for anti-aliasing and frequency attention~\cite{wavecnet,fcanet}; dynamic token methods allocate computation by input~\cite{dynamicvit}. These lines motivate WSR but also expose a systems caveat: scoring, sorting, indexing, and memory movement can dominate the skipped arithmetic. Our design therefore separates algorithmic sparsity from measured speed.

\section{Wavelet-Conditioned Sparse Routing}
Let $\mathbf X\in\mathbb R^{C\times H\times W}$ be the P3/8 feature, split into wave and refinement tensors $\mathbf X_w,\mathbf X_r$. Fixed grouped stride-two Haar convolution gives
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{LL},\mathbf{LH},\mathbf{HL},\mathbf{HH})=\mathcal H(\mathbf X_w).
\end{equation}
Channel-averaged detail energy $E_{hf}$, low-frequency magnitude $E_{ll}$, and a local residual $G_{ll}$ are normalized and passed to a small router:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf S=R_\theta([\bar E_{hf};\bar E_{ll};\bar G_{ll}])
+\log(1+\bar E_{hf}+\bar G_{ll}).
\end{equation}
Flattening spatial locations, the router selects an exact budget
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I=\operatorname{TopK}(\mathbf S,k),\quad
k=\min(HW,\max(4,\operatorname{round}(\rho HW))).
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:wsr} summarizes the data and control paths.

A shared softmax-normalized $3\times3$ kernel provides context over $\mathbf X_r$; only indices $\mathcal I$ are gathered. For token tensor $\mathbf T$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf T'_{\mathcal I}=\mathbf T_{\mathcal I}+\sigma(\mathbf S_{\mathcal I})\odot
\operatorname{MLP}\!\left(\operatorname{LN}(\mathbf T_{\mathcal I}+\gamma(\tilde{\mathbf T}_{\mathcal I}-\mathbf T_{\mathcal I}))\right).
\end{equation}
Unrouted tokens remain unchanged. A two-way softmax gate fuses the wave and refinement branches with an identity skip. The formal model inserts one block at P3 with $\rho=0.25$; hard indices receive no gradient, while selected confidence factors and detector losses train the router.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Protocol}
DsPCBSD+ has 10,259 images and 20,276 boxes. Its 8,208 training images are split 7,387/821 for train/validation, and the official 2,051-image validation set is locked as test. DeepPCB uses 850/150 training/validation targets and all 500 official test targets; clean templates are audited separately. Both models start from the same YOLO11s checkpoint and use 640-pixel input, at most 80 epochs, deterministic SGD, matched augmentation, and paired seeds. Seven DsPCBSD+ pairs form the primary analysis; three DeepPCB pairs are descriptive. One pinned COCO evaluator~\cite{coco} reports AP, and batch-one RTX 3090 timing uses synchronized ABBA/BAAB cycles.

Before training, we validate paths, categories, boxes, file hashes, repeated pseudo-boxes, and perceptual similarity. Table~\ref{tab:data} reports the resulting frozen partitions. No exact cross-split duplicate is found, although repeated layouts yield conservative similarity candidates and missing board/lot identifiers prevent a categorical leakage-free claim. Checkpoints below 99\% pretrained-key transfer are rejected. The P3/25\% model is selected using validation data before test access; later component, robustness, and false-alarm studies remain explicitly diagnostic or exploratory and cannot replace the registered comparison.

For selected cells $M_i$, rasterized box cells $B_i$, and grid size $N_i$, route recall is $|M_i\cap B_i|/|B_i|$ and enrichment divides that recall by $|M_i|/N_i$. Board-FPR is the fraction of clean templates with at least one retained detection. Paired seeds are the sampling unit for accuracy; latency cycles are the unit for device timing.

\subsection{Routing, Controls, and Cost}
At 25\%, learned routes recall 79.11\%/88.68\% of box cells on DsPCBSD+/DeepPCB, corresponding to 3.164$\times$/3.547$\times$ enrichment. Figure~\ref{fig:context_route} shows that center and training-occupancy priors explain much of the DsPCBSD+ enrichment, whereas learned routing is stronger on DeepPCB. Cross-image shuffling lowers enrichment, confirming image dependence without proving wavelet uniqueness. Full-data matched convolution ties WSR; equal fusion and removal of HF cues do not reduce the three-seed validation mean. Thus capacity and optimization remain plausible explanations.

The comparison includes uniform random, center-prior, training-occupancy, activation-energy, fixed-Haar, learned-WSR, and cross-image-shuffled routes at the same budget. This matters because high overlap is otherwise trivial when many cells are selected, and PCB layouts place defects nonuniformly. The registered validation gate admitted WSR at +1.283 AP points, 2.469$\times$ enrichment, and a preliminary 0.982 latency ratio; locked device timing did not reproduce the apparent speed advantage. Selection-stage timing is therefore not reported as test evidence.

Across 27 full-data validation jobs, WSR reaches 48.025 AP versus 46.995 for the repeated baseline, with paired interval $[-0.158,2.219]$. Equal fusion, removal of HF cues, and matched convolution reach 48.314, 48.296, and 48.057 AP, respectively. These controls reproduce the positive validation trend without uniquely attributing it to wavelet cues or adaptive fusion.

\subsection{Operational False Alarms}
DeepPCB's 500 clean templates expose behavior hidden by positive-only AP. Table~\ref{tab:fpr_sweep} shows that seed-13 WSR has higher board-FPR than YOLO11s at every audited threshold. At confidence 0.25, WSR alarms on 56.6\% of boards (1.472 FP/image) versus 48.0\% (1.138). Three negative-aware stages reduce the three-seed board-FPR from 53.5\% to 27.5\% at 0.946 recall while increasing AP from 68.81 to 72.44. Figure~\ref{fig:false_alarm} also shows an exploratory paired-reference policy that reaches 7/500 alarms (1.4\%), AP 72.63, and 0.959 F1. It is a feasibility result because it is post-hoc, single-seed, and changes the input contract.

The target-only stages add clean-template negatives, tune class thresholds on validation data, and calibrate the final operating point without exposing test labels. The paired alternative encodes an aligned candidate and reference through a clipped residual representation before adaptation with synthetic negatives. Its much lower board-FPR is operationally important but not directly comparable with target-only detection: it assumes a registered clean template at inference and is evaluated on perturbations of known templates. Independent production negatives stratified by camera, illumination, layout, and lot are still required.

Stage 1 uses a frozen 25\% subset of training templates as empty-label backgrounds; Stage 2 uses all 850 training templates; Stage 3 oversamples the highest-scoring training-template quartile. The seed-13 schedule is then frozen for seeds 42 and 3407. Validation-calibrated 1\% caps transfer to 1.8\%--6.2\% test board-FPR with mean recall 0.790, showing that threshold elevation alone is unstable.

\subsection{Auxiliary Robustness Evidence}
Across eight corruptions and five severities on DeepPCB seed 13, WSR averages 46.51 AP versus 42.68 and wins 24 of 40 conditions. Gains concentrate in brightness, contrast, Gaussian noise, and JPEG; defocus, Poisson, and speckle noise regress. Clean-normalized retention is 65.6\% versus 64.1\%, indicating conditional rather than general robustness.

Input-Haar interventions give 67.43/66.22 AP for WSR/baseline under low-only reconstruction and 0.95/1.67 under high-only reconstruction. Removing LH, HL, or HH costs WSR 1.32, 0.70, or 1.68 points, respectively. Because Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} reuses a single checkpoint and modifies the detector input, it is mechanistic evidence rather than proof that the internal Haar branch causes the accuracy trend.

\section{Findings and Limitations}
Route enrichment is not an accuracy surrogate, arithmetic sparsity is not systems efficiency, and positive-only AP hides normal-board alarms. Evaluation must therefore combine paired held-out accuracy, synchronized device timing, negative operating points, same-budget controls, and matched capacity. Manifests, hashes, seeds, and source-bound records separate registered tests from later diagnostics; class, corruption, and threshold slices are decompositions rather than new samples.

\subsection{Image-Processing Role and Deployment Gate}
Filtering, binarization, and edge operators remain useful for deterministic normalization, registration, and ROI masking, but fixed thresholds are brittle under illumination shifts and repeated copper patterns. WSR-YOLO learns multiscale context; its Haar router allocates computation rather than acting as a fixed edge detector. A release freezes classwise thresholds under a validation board-FPR ceiling, then reports AP$_{50:95}$, recall, board-FPR uncertainty, and end-to-end latency on the locked test. Camera, illumination, or layout changes trigger recalibration, and loss of the registered template disables the paired branch explicitly.

Evidence is limited by three DeepPCB pairs, one GPU, incomplete comparator timing, hard top-$k$, absent board/lot IDs, and unresolved similarity candidates. Reference assistance needs preregistration and independent production negatives. Runs bind Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.5.1/CUDA 12.1, Ultralytics 8.4.50, source revision, split manifest, seed, checkpoint, and evaluator output; validation controls never read test labels.

\section{Conclusion}
WSR concentrates selected P3 cells inside defects at little arithmetic cost, yet establishes neither a reliable primary accuracy gain nor acceleration, and target-only false alarms remain high. Its contribution is an auditable image-processing result and evaluation template rather than a superiority claim. Future work should test fused sparse kernels, more paired seeds, group-safe PCB splits, established attention controls, and independent reference-assisted evaluation.
